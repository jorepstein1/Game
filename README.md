# Hearthold

A medieval idle city builder that runs entirely in the browser. No build step and no dependencies: open `index.html` (or serve the folder with any static server).

## How it plays

- **Place buildings on the map.** Position matters: lumber camps want forests, quarries and mines want hills, farms want water and mills, markets want cottages, taverns boost cottages. Bonus badges preview what each plot would give before you build. Terrain comes in features (groves, hill ranges, a lake or a river) with open plots in their bays and clearings, so every map has double and triple sites (plots touching two or three tiles of one kind); the starting land always has a double forest site, and each expansion reveals at least one more triple site and three double ones.
- **Balance the village.** Cottages house villagers (a flat amount per level), working buildings need them, and everyone eats. Food upkeep per villager rises as the town grows, so housing and farms have to keep pace with everything else. Villagers only move in while there is food for them; if the stores run dry, work slows to half and villagers drift away until the farms can feed the rest.
- **Upgrade by kind and expand.** An upgrade raises every building of a kind at once: tap any Farm and "Upgrade all Farms to level 4" (on a phone, "Upgrade all to level 4" under the Farm's name) lifts every Farm you have, and new Farms are built at level 4 too. Each level triples a building's output (cottages, farms and mills gain their level-1 amount again instead), adds its level-1 jobs again, and a neighbour's bonus grows with the square root of its kind's level. The button says what the level does (output ×3, the flat gain, the extra housing and jobs). Raise your Village Hall into a Manor, Keep, Castle and Palace, one level at a time and always by hand, to unlock new buildings and research, and buy more land as you grow. When the map is full, forest, hills and water can be cleared into open plots for gold.
- **One price rule.** What a kind has cost in all depends only on how many stand and at what level: each further copy costs more than the last (steeply so past the first few Lumber Camps, Quarries, Farms and Cottages), the price of a new one rises with the kind's level, and an upgrade costs more the more copies it raises. Building first or upgrading first comes to the same total. Demolishing refunds three quarters of what building that copy again would cost, so a layout can be changed for a modest price and nothing can be gained by building and demolishing. The rule and its numbers are written out in `data.js`.
- **Follow the Decrees.** Goals with rewards that double as the tutorial. Three are open at a time: work on whichever you like and pin the one you are pursuing; none expires, and claiming one brings the next. One of the early ones, A Full Treasury, is about saving for the Manor.
- **The next hall is always in view.** The Build tab opens with the next hall's price and how long the town needs to afford it at today's income, and the welcome-back report says the same. Build and research cards say what the purchase adds per second, and a build card says when new ones arrive above level 1 (a phone's build bar shows that level as a badge). Until the first kind upgrade the Build tab says that upgrades are made by tapping a building. While the hall is within twelve hours of income, build cards and the upgrade button say whether a purchase delays it or brings it closer. The same card says where the rested bonus stands and, once abdication is open, how many Crowns are on offer. Monuments start at 40M gold, so there is something to buy at a Palace while the Crowns build up.
- **A game of check-ins.** The pace is set for a few visits a day. Time away counts in full, so an open game earns no more than a closed one: there is no merchant to catch and no festival, and Collect Taxes pays a single coin, a flourish for the first minutes: it leaves the screen once the Manor stands or once 100 gold has come in by hand that reign, so tapping cannot buy a hall sooner. With five check-ins a day a first reign reaches the Manor in about a quarter of an hour, the Keep in about 4 hours, the Castle in about 12 and the Palace on the evening of the second day (37 to 41 hours), with a town of 30 to 40 buildings; a check-in is a handful of purchases. A rested town works harder: after the first hour of time away without a purchase, each further hour counts as 2.5 hours, and past eight hours (a night) as 4.5 hours, all within the offline cap. A 4-hour absence is worth 8.5 hours, a night of 8 hours 18.5. Rest is counted since the last purchase, not since the last visit, so looking in without buying costs nothing, and an open game never earns it. The welcome-back summary says how long you were away and what it counted as, and the next-hall card says in one sentence whether the town is rested and why. In the balance bot's hands a week at two check-ins a day ends with about 85% of the Crowns of five a day, 49 a day with about the same as five, and a game that is never closed with less. `tests/balance.test.js` prints the figures.
- **Found a new kingdom (prestige).** You can abdicate for Crowns, each a permanent +5% to all production. Crowns on offer are `floor(13 × log10(gold earned this reign / threshold))`, where the threshold is 1.4M gold, times `(Crown multiplier / 2.5)^4` once more than 30 Crowns are held. Abdication needs a Castle and at least 15 Crowns on offer, which a first reign reaches about when its Palace stands; smaller offers are not made. The Realm tab says whether the offer is small or good, the tab's dot lights at 20 Crowns (about half a day after the Palace), and the confirmation warns about a small offer. Later reigns take a day or two each: about 90 Crowns after a week at five check-ins a day. A new kingdom begins with a few extra supplies (250 gold, 200 wood, 60 food, 40 stone), enough to skip the first steps and no more.
- **The Palace ends a first reign.** Two grander halls follow it, the Royal Palace and the Imperial Palace, but they open only with Crowns held (30 and 75, not Crowns on offer) and each costs 20× the hall before, so later reigns end somewhere new; there is no hall past the Imperial Palace, and every reign still starts at a Village Hall. Until then the hall panel says what is left to do in the reign: Monuments, then abdicating.
- **Repeatable upgrades.** From the Keep onwards the Research tab offers gold sinks with exponentially scaling costs: food, housing and all-production bonuses, plus Monuments that each add a Crown to your next abdication. They reset when you found a new kingdom.
- **Idle.** The game saves every 10 seconds and credits up to 8 hours of time away, or 24 with Stewardship (a Village Hall research costing 200 gold, kept when you found a new kingdom), whether the page was closed or the tab was asleep.
- **Phones.** On narrow screens the game is an app-style screen that never scrolls as a page: the resource bar (a single compact row) on top, the map, a strip with what is selected (for a building, just its numbers: the bar names it) or the current decree, beside Collect Taxes until the Manor, a bar holding the build list (or the selected building's upgrade-all button and Demolish), and the tab row along the bottom. A tab opens its panel as a sheet over the lower part of the screen, with its own scrolling; tapping the tab again, the sheet's close button or a tile closes it. The map is as large as the shorter side of the space left for it, so a small map is always whole; plots never drop below 40px, and a map too big for that pans inside its frame. A phone on its side puts the map on the left and the controls on the right.

## Saves

Saves live in the browser's `localStorage` and can be exported and imported as text from the Realm tab. Each save carries a version number (`v`), the terrain grid and the current decree by id. Loading rebuilds the game from known fields only, coercing and clamping every value, so older saves are migrated and damaged ones cannot break the page; a save that cannot be read at all is kept under a backup key and a new kingdom is started. Only one tab plays at a time: opening the game in a second tab pauses the first.

## Files

Plain script tags, loaded in this order:

- `index.html` — page shell: one `<section class="screen">` per game mode inside `#app`
- `style.css` — presentation
- `data.js` — data tables and balance constants (buildings, research, repeatable upgrades, decrees, costs)
- `sprites.js` — the art: original vector sprites drawn on a 32×32 grid, one entry per sprite in `SPRITES`. They are injected once as hidden SVG symbols, and `sprite(id, label)` returns a small `<svg><use>` that sizes with the text around it. In the browser it replaces the plain-text `icon` of every resource, terrain, hall level and building in the data tables with its sprite. To add one, add an entry (shapes inherit a dark rounded outline) and call `sprite('its-id')`
- `sim.js` — state (`state.meta` survives abdication, `state.run` is the current city), rules, actions and the save format. No DOM, so it also runs in Node: load `data.js` then `sim.js` into one `vm` context, call `startNewGame(0)` and drive it with `advance(seconds)` and the action functions
- `ui.js` — rendering, input, timers and storage

## Tests

```
node tests/sim.test.js        # rules and save format, in Node (no dependencies)
node tests/bot.js             # balance bot: a first reign of 72 hours at five check-ins a day on seeds 11, 22, 33 (no dependencies, seconds)
node tests/bot.js 240 crowns:20 11 2   # arguments: hours, abdication policy, comma-separated seeds, check-ins per day (0 = game always open)
node tests/bot.js compare 168 11,22,33,44,55,66   # 2, 5 and 49 check-ins a day and a game never closed, a week abdicating at 20 Crowns (a few minutes)
node tests/bot.js 60 none 11,22,33,44,55,66 5 log # first reign on six maps, with every check-in's purchases listed
node tests/balance.test.js    # holds the game to the pacing design: hall and Crown times, the opening, the town at the Palace, what checking in more often is worth (several minutes); GAP lines are targets not met yet
node tests/ui.test.js         # layout and input in headless Chromium (needs Playwright: npm i playwright)
```

`tests/harness.js` loads `data.js` and `sim.js` into a `vm` context with no DOM. The bot is deterministic: the same arguments print the same numbers. It plays in check-ins (time away is credited through `creditAway`, as on a page load) stays for an opening session whenever it founds a new kingdom, and reports purchases per check-in and payback by stage; how it decides is described at the top of `tests/bot.js`.
